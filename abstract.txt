essentialism is a generative artwork of 44 editions whose program is stored on the Tezos blockchain. The program carries two of my photographs as 28x38 grids of 16 gray levels. Each token draws one of them, blended with noise, at a weight set by its edition number: 100% at edition 1, falling by equal steps to 12% at edition 44. The token prints that weight as its "memory". I measured how much of the photograph survives into the drawing, over 1,000 seeds at every edition. The photograph's median correlation with the generator's internal darkness field stays above 0.94 through edition 21, and more than half of its total decline falls between editions 34 and 44; a closed form with two measured spreads reproduces that curve to within 0.021. In the ink of the drawn dots, the median rank correlation with the photograph falls from 0.82 at edition 1 to 0.14 at edition 44 for the tree (the sky runs 0.84 to 0.13), against a control near zero. The ledger, meanwhile, kept every write: every superseded version of the program, including a first version whose header claimed an algorithm that was not mine, and the record each mint wrote, whose render matches the platform's own image of every minted token at a gray-level correlation of 0.98 to 0.99. This is a study of one work by its author, and its measurements describe what the program draws at every edition, thirty of which are not yet minted, not what a viewer recognizes. Code, data and this paper's source rebuild from one command; they are archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23333265 and maintained at https://github.com/TheBenMeadows/essentialism-preprint.
